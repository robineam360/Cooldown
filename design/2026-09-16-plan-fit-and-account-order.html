CCRM-70 (Plan Fit) & CCRM-71 (Account Order) · 2026-09-16

Two History & Settings changes: a plan-fit reading, and dragging accounts into order

CCRM-70 (Plan Fit) adds a tonal block above the weekly bars on the History screen's 7-day pane — a reading of whether the plan you're on fits eight weeks of your own usage, with no dollar figure and no red. CCRM-71 (Account Order) adds a drag-to-reorder sheet for the Accounts list in Settings, reached from a new Reorder button, because the account order now drives three surfaces (the tab strip, app shortcuts, the notification's overflow strip) with no way to change it today.

For review before any code is written — working agreement 2 (wireframe first). Not yet approved.

1 · Plan Fit — anatomy

A tonal block (Material3 surfaceVariant, never an accent or warning colour) inserted directly above WeeklyView in the 7-day pane. It displaces nothing — the weekly bars simply move down by the block's own height, ~104 dp including the gap beneath it. Four possible text lines, all left-aligned, none of them ever red.

Why never red, ever — not even for "runs out" or "capped every week". This block is a reading, not an alarm: it fires once a week at most and answers a question the user asked by opening History, not one the phone is interrupting them to raise. "This plan runs out on you" rendered in red would read as an accusation aimed at how the person works, not a system state to react to — and there is no action to take from this screen anyway (CCRM-70 (Plan Fit) is explicit that it never names another tier to move to). The weekly bars directly below keep their existing red/orange/yellow severity colouring unchanged — that convention already existed for a different purpose (a single window that is close to or at its cap) and this block doesn't touch it.

9 · Annotations

  • The Plan Fit block never uses red or any warning colour, anywhere, in any state — including "This plan runs out on you" and "Hit the cap in 8 of 8 weeks." It's a reading the user opened History to see, not an interruption; it names no action and no other tier to move to. Elaborated in §1. The weekly bars directly beneath it keep their existing severity colours (yellow/orange/red as a window nears its own cap) unchanged — that's a different, pre-existing convention this feature doesn't touch.
  • Reordering accounts also changes app-shortcut order and the notification panel's overflow fault-strip order (notify/Conditions.kt:80-94 reads registry order), and registry.first() stays the fallback for a key that no longer resolves. Both were judged too minor to surface as their own row in the sheet or Settings — disclosed here instead, per CCRM-71 (Account Order)'s own roadmap entry.
  • Main and History must key the selected tab on the account's stable profile.key, never on its position in the list. Demonstrated in §8: the same drag that makes the sheet feel obviously correct (Side is now first) silently changes which account's numbers are on screen if selection ever falls back to an index. Profile.slot (notification IDs, alarm request codes) and per-account accents are already keyed this way; tab selection is the one place that has to join them.
