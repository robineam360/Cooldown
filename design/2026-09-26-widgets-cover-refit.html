CCBG-38 (Cover Buckets) · CCRM-78 (Widgets Reborn) · 2026-09-26 · rev F

The faces, refitted to the frames the Fold 7 actually hands them

Rev D fitted every face to a cover cell of 90.75×84 dp. The Step 7 device pass measured One UI's real frames, and they are narrower and much taller: a 2×2 on Robin's cover is 156×237 dp, not 181×168. Every 2- and 4-column frame came in under its size-map key, so Android fell back to each face's smallest layout — CCBG-38 (Cover Buckets). Rev F keeps every rev D face exactly as approved and changes only how a face meets its frame: the bucket picks the layout, the reported frame sets the geometry.

Rev F.1 — approved by Fable on Robin's delegation, 2026-09-26, after two rounds: its seven required changes and four of its suggestions are folded in and marked "F.1".

What changes from rev D
  • Size map keyed by the reported frames. The provider reads OPTION_APPWIDGET_SIZES and hands the launcher one layout per reported frame, drawn at that frame and keyed 2 dp under it (F.1: the host matches against its own laid-out size with only +1 dp of slack, so a half-dp rounding on One UI's scaled host must not drop the widget to the smallest entry again). A Fold that reports a cover and an inner frame gets both.
  • R10 with frames (F.1). Frames are taken smallest first while the composed map's bitmaps stay under R10's 2 MB, never more than four (a Pixel Fold reports two screens × two orientations) and always at least one; when the budget or the cap is hit, the largest are dropped. The host falls back to its smallest entry when no key fits, and a smaller face centred in a larger frame is whole, where the reverse would clip. At 2.625 dpi a Ø150 ring is 0.62 MB, so a Fold's two Ring frames fit together.
  • Before any frame is reported (F.1) — the very first update — each class is drawn at its threshold frame and keyed there, so no bitmap is ever wider than the frame that picks it: Ring 1×1 84×84, Ring 2×2 140×140, Number and Countdown 2×1 140×84, Number 4×1 240×84, 4×2 240×150, Countdown 2×2 140×150, Strip 4×1 244×84, 4×2 244×150. The launcher's first options broadcast redraws at the real frame.
  • Class thresholds, not exact cells. Ring: 2×2 layout once the frame is ≥140 dp both ways, else 1×1. Number: 2×1 under 240 dp wide; 4×1 from 240 dp; 4×2 from 240×150 (F.1: the cover's 3-column 244 dp frame has the width for the sub-line). Countdown: 2×2 from 140×150, else 2×1. Strip: 4×2 from 150 dp tall, else 4×1.
  • Geometry from the frame. Ring Ø is the inner width or the inner height less the label row, whichever is smaller (1×1 capped at 64, 2×2 at 150); stroke and figure scale with it from rev D's 110 / 9 dp / 26 sp. Bars and label clamps use the frame's inner width. Content centres vertically in a frame taller than it needs.
  • Padding from the frame: 8 dp top and bottom under a 90 dp row and 8 dp a side under 90 dp wide (F.1, so the cover's 84 dp 1×1 keeps Ø64), 14 dp once both sides reach 200 dp, 12 dp otherwise — rev D's values, now chosen by size, not by a list of known cells.
  • Countdown 2×2's estimate (F.1): when "62% ~ runs out 7:54 PM" does not fit one line and the frame is ≥150 dp tall, the estimate takes its own 11 sp line under the figure, above the bar.
  • The "as of" stamp gives way, never the name (F.1): on the Ring 2×2 and the Countdown 2×2, when the account line and the stamp do not fit one line, the stamp is left off.
  • Number 2×1 on a narrow short frame (F.1): under 150 dp inner and under 100 dp tall (a five-column launcher) the label is the name alone, rev D's compact rule, instead of an ellipsis.
  • Countdown 2×1 on a narrow frame: when "2:41:07 at 9:10 PM" does not fit one line, "at 9:10 PM" drops to its own line under the count. One UI's 108 dp row has the height; the Pixel's 84 dp row keeps rev D's one line.
  • Picker previews are one static layout each, not a size map (F.1): the Ring's ring takes the height the preview box leaves above its label, centred; each Strip ring takes the height its label leaves — §6.
What does not change: every face's content, order, type sizes on the rev D frames, colours, backgrounds, the pace tick, the synthetic ribbon and dot, the states table, the taps.

1 · Every face on the cover, at the frames One UI gives it

S1 (normal), Solid, dark and light. Each tile is drawn at the reported frame, 1 px = 1 dp; the caption names the grid cells, the frame and the layout the class rule picks. The first tile of each face is its default placement.

Ring 2×1 (156×108) is shorter than 140 dp, so it takes the 1×1 layout, centred — a ring with no label, the same as rev D's 1×1. Number at 2×2 is under 300 dp wide, so it keeps the 2×1 layout, centred in the taller frame; rev D has no Number layout for a square frame and rev F does not invent one.

2 · States at the tightest cover frames

The states most likely to overflow — long labels, the widest figures, the markers — at the narrowest frame each face can have on the cover. A run that overflows its box draws a red outline with the overflow in px (none should). Dark Solid; S4 uses ChatGPT.

F.1 · The "as of" stamp, a five-account Strip, a five-column launcher

The stamp where rev D draws it, at the narrow cover frames: it stays when the line has room and is left off when it does not — the account name is never the one to go. The Strip with five accounts shows four rings and a "+1" cell. A five-column launcher's 2×1 (~140×84 dp) drops the window suffix, rev D's compact rule.

3 · The inner screen

The inner frames already fit rev D's keys; rev F draws them at their own size too. 2×2 and 4×3 are measured; the one-row height is derived from the row pitch (confirm at the re-run).

4 · Rev D's frames still draw rev D

The same rule at the frames rev D was fitted to (the Pixel launcher's 84 dp rows): nothing moves. This is the check that rev F is a refit, not a redesign.

5 · Transparent on the cover

The tall frames on a wallpaper, both themes, including the mismatched case — the centred content must still read with rev D's soft shadow.

6 · Picker previews (F.1)

A preview is one static layout (Solid, dark, S1), so it cannot pick a layout by size. It is drawn here in the boxes One UI's picker gave it: the Ring's ring takes the height its box leaves above the label, centred, so a narrow box never clips it, and its figure auto-sizes inside the bore (26 sp at most); each Strip ring takes the height its label leaves, so a short box shrinks the rings rather than cutting the names.

7 · Decisions

Fable approved these with the F.1 changes above. Two are visible departures from rev D and are named for Robin: the stacked Number 2×1 (label above the figure, figure left) and the larger Ring 2×2 (Ø131 / 30 sp on the cover, against rev D's Ø110 / 26 sp).

  1. Key on the reported frame (one layout per entry in OPTION_APPWIDGET_SIZES, drawn at that size), rather than retuning the fixed keys to the Fold's numbers. Why: One UI's frames change with the grid setting and differ from the Pixel's; any fixed table breaks on the next grid.
  2. The class thresholds in the box at the top (140 / 150 / 240 dp). Why: they sit below the smallest frame each class gets on the Fold cover (156 wide, 237 tall, 244 wide) and above the largest frame of the class below it.
  3. Ring Ø grows with the frame, capped at 150 dp; on the cover 2×2 that is Ø131 with a 31 sp figure, against rev D's Ø110 / 26 sp. Alternative: keep Ø110 and leave the extra space empty — rejected, it strands the ring in a tall card as the device pass showed.
  4. Extra height stays empty, centred. No new line appears just because a frame is taller; adding content would be a design change beyond this bug.
  5. Countdown 2×1 stacks "at 9:10 PM" under the count when one line does not fit and the frame has the height; otherwise rev D's one line and its ellipsis.

For Robin (Fable): One UI draws every widget at hsResizeRatio — 0.714 on the cover — so every type size lands at about 71%: the Strip 4×1's 9.5 / 8 sp reads as ~6.8 / 5.7 sp, under rev C's 8 sp floor. The app cannot read that ratio. Whether the Strip's type moves up on frames 108 dp or taller is Robin's call; rev F leaves rev D's sizes.
