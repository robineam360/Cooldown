1b · Percent sign: baseline or top-aligned

Robin: "reduce the size of the percentage so the number becomes the central piece and not the symbol along with it." The "%" at 58% of the digits' size, at three sizes: the 44 sp expanded-notification figure, the 17 sp figure on a bar's row, and a 36 dp ring centre. Recommended: baseline, used on every mock on this page. It reads as one number with a small unit, keeps the figure's bottom edge level with the label beside it, and keeps a ring's figure optically centred. Top-aligned reads as a footnote mark, and in a 36 dp ring it climbs toward the band.

1c · The figure font (rev C)

Robin: "the percentage number font used is weird and it makes each number not fit within the same vertical height. Use a proper neat readable font." Rev B set the bar and Summary figures in Georgia, whose old-style digits rise and drop (3, 4, 5, 7 and 9 hang below the line, 6 and 8 climb). Rev C sets every figure (ring centres, bar figures, notification, widgets, Summary, iOS) in one sans with tabular, lining figures: Android Roboto with tnum and lnum, iOS SF Pro with monospaced digits. Every digit sits on the baseline at cap height and takes the same width, so figures stacked in a column line up. The small "%" (58%, baseline) stays. The ring figures are re-fitted to the bore with the tabular widths (fit log below).

1d · The dashed room on the rings (rev C)

Rev B drew the dashed room (between the usage and the pace mark) on bars only. Rev C draws it on every ring: the arc segment from the fill's end to the pace tick, outlined dashed in the account colour at reduced alpha, open at the fill's end as on the bar. It shows only under the mark; over it, the red slice shows instead. Every ring on this page now carries it. Sizes from 96 dp down; under 28 dp it is dropped.

1e · The usage end mark: E0 to E3 (rev C)

Today (E0) the fill ends in a round cap, which also draws the fill half a band further than the usage: about 2.7% of the circle on a 72 dp ring, so near the mark the cap can look past the tick when the usage is not. The variants end the fill square at the usage and add a short perpendicular line, thinner and shorter than the pace tick (bar: 1.5 dp, 1.5 dp past each edge; ring: 0.35 of the band past each edge), with a hairline gap in the surface colour so it stays visible on the red slice. E2 colours the line by pace state, Vs pace's green and red, with two readings of "near": E2a on the mark (|d| < 1 point), E2b the ±5 on-pace band of the band words. E3 keeps the line neutral and puts a pace-coloured dot on it. Fixtures: under 34% against a 53% mark; near 50% against 52% (2 under: green in E2a, ink in E2b); over 64% against 52%. In each cell: tile size (ring 72, bar 10 dp) above, notification size (ring 44, bar 13 dp) below.

2b · Tap a figure: when the pace mark meets the usage (rev C)

spec/core §6.3 "When the mark meets the usage" (fixtures pace-distance/cases.json → markTime): the pace mark passes the usage at windowStart + used ÷ 100 × windowLength. Over pace, tapping the figure shows that instant, ahead: "Pace catches up Thu 5 Nov, 12:50 pm" (absolute day, date and time). Under pace, it shows how far the usage trails, as a duration: "1d 4h behind pace" (Xd Yh; under a day Xh Ym, under an hour Ym, all rounded down). On the mark (< 1 point): "On the pace mark". Spent, no clock or no reading: the tap does nothing. The page's moment is Wed 4 Nov, 2:50 pm. The tap target is the figure, the ring's figure included (on the ring the tap also selects the 5h chip, rev B). It works in every Usage display mode: the readout is about the usage, whatever the figure shows. Three forms: (a) a popover above the figure, (b) the figure's own line swaps its text for 3 s (a bar's caption, the ring's 5h line, a Summary row's line 3), (c) an always-on extra line. Recommended: (b).
