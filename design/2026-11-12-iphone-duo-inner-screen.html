iPhone Duo inner screen — a layout of its own

APPROVED by Robin 2026-11-12 2026-11-12 · wireframe for CCBG-90 (Inner Portrait Stretch), RUNBOOK Step 3 · built as CCRM-116 (Inner Panes); Android panes are CCRM-117 (Fold Panes). Baseline: design/2026-11-11-iphone-duo-pass.html “Unfolded” U1–U6, spec/mobile/surfaces/.

The idea in one line. On the inner screen every page is two panes: Usage and Pace. Wide, they sit side by side; tall, they stack. Half open, the divider lands on the fold, so no text sits on it. Flat, it is the same two panes split evenly. No pt breakpoints: the rule is “regular width → two panes, axis by aspect”, which is what Apple’s arrangement view (split) does and what its Duo guidance asks for.

Decisions (all answered 2026-11-12)

  1. The concept — approved by Robin 2026-11-12: two panes on every inner-screen page, same views flat and half open (§3–§4).
  2. Tall: title — approved: moves into the top bar (buys 44 pt, so 3 accounts fit the top half) (§3b).
  3. Account tab — approved: the tile splits into a usage card and a chart card; fold toggle and chart-height setting do not apply (§5).
  4. History — approved: side by side wide; 5-hour on top, 7-day below when tall (§6).
  5. iPad — approved, follows the same rule instead of keeping 700 / 900 / 600 pt (§9).
  6. CCBG-86 (Rail Marks Alike): in as B + C (accent-coloured mark plus tag glyph; Robin’s pick, §9a).

1 · What Apple says, and what it settles

  • Do not design a layout per pose; use size classes. A compact layout for the outer display plus a regular layout for the inner covers every pose. Avoid fixed widths.
  • Half open, the fold is a reserved region that divides the inner display into usable regions; prefer adaptive containers, even column counts, and small adjustments while folding.
  • Arrangement view (new): a primary and a secondary view; a split divides horizontally when wider than tall and vertically when taller than wide, and moves to the fold when partly open. Navigation stays outside it.
  • Inner screen turned tall keeps standard horizontal bars (labelled tab bar at the bottom); wide, the bars sit in a rail on the right. The app does not draw either.

So Robin’s three ideas are one idea: Usage + Pace as a split, with the axis following the screen and the divider following the fold.

3 · The main page

3a · Wide, 3 accounts — flat and half open

  • Usage pane: the Weekly header (All · Personal · Work, sort) and the rows exactly as built; Checked 4m ago ↻ at the end of its scroll; .refreshable here. It scrolls on its own.
  • Pace pane: today’s Pace map card, chips and all; it does not scroll. Plot height follows the pane (≤ 1.15 × width, so it never turns into a tall sliver); spare room below stays empty.
  • Title (34 pt) stays top left as today; the rail is the system’s.
  • Row glyph. The person / briefcase leads the provider mark on every row, full and one-line (CCRM-115 (Row Tag Glyph), 14 pt box + 4 before the mark; 12 pt on one-line rows). My first drawing left it off the full rows; corrected here.

3b · Tall, 3 accounts — flat and half open

  • Title in the top bar. “Cooldown” drops to 28 pt in the bar’s free left side (the bar has a 363 pt gap there); without it the top pane starts at y 130 and the 3-row card (≈ 357 pt) ends 23 pt below the fold. With it, it ends 16 pt above.
  • Pace pane ends above the floating tab bar; plot 629 × ≈ 240, the same height the stretched phone page gives today.
  • Flat vs half open: the divider moves ≤ 12 pt; the only change is the gap.

3c · 10 accounts, with a spent window

Wide: ten one-line rows (≈ 431 pt) fit the 597 pt pane with no scrolling; the glyph column stays (a 405 pt row keeps the bar over 50 pt).

Tall: 431 pt of card against a 373.5 pt pane: it scrolls by 57 pt on its own and the map never moves. Pull-to-refresh belongs to this pane.

Spent (Atlas): row dimmed with “Out · Fri 1:48 PM” in red, × at 100% on the map, as built.

Map names follow the existing drop rule: a name with no free spot is not drawn; its dot still pins the callout.

6 · History

  • Same rule as the main page: wide → 5-hour | 7-day side by side; tall → 5-hour on top, 7-day below, so the fold falls between them. Today’s tall History goes two columns at 669 pt, which is what disagreed with the main page.
  • The 5-hour card is long (≈ 387 pt); on a tall inner screen it scrolls on its own in the top pane. The 7-day pane (Plan Fit + card ≈ 230 pt) fits whole.
  • No Plan Fit and no 5-hour window (weekly-only plan): one pane, centred in the area, no empty second pane.
  • Back button and “Usage history” inline in the bar, as on the main page. The footnote closes the 7-day pane, so it never sits on the fold or under the tab bar.
  • The account strip stays above the panes at the column width (it ends at the rail, which also covers CCBG-87 (Strip Under Rail)); label sizing, CCBG-88 (Tab Strip Truncates), stays separate.

9 · Scope

  • CCBG-86 (Rail Marks Alike): out of scope. It is the system’s icon-only rail (“Native everywhere”, Robin 2026-11-02) and the tracker still wants a decision before a wireframe. It shows only on the wide inner screen; tall has a labelled bar. This layout does not add or change a tab, and in All every row and map dot opens its account in one tap, a second route.
  • iPad follows the same rule. Apple asks for no device-tied widths, and a Duo-only rule would need a device check. Consequence: iPad portrait gets Usage over Pace (each ≈ half of the height); iPad landscape keeps the sidebar (≥ 1000 pt today) and shows its detail column as Usage | Pace. Account pages lose the “header over two cards” arrangement in the same way as §5. Split View too narrow for regular width keeps the phone page. iPad captures are part of the build pass.
  • Not changed: the cover/phone page, the system chrome, cards’ content and wording, Settings and its sheets (form sheets already read well, U2), widgets.

B + C (Robin’s pick). Accent-coloured mark with the person / briefcase badge. Initials (A) are dropped. Limit: Teams, Lab and Atlas are all Claude and all Work, so the glyph does not separate them; only an accent set per account does.

Where the colour comes from. The account’s accent has three levels (app.md §1.1): the account’s own accent (Settings › account › Accent colour…), else the global Theme colour unless “Per provider”, else the provider colour. So the per-account theme colour exists on iOS and Android; the rail just follows it.

Feasibility of C. The system draws tab icons as template images in its own tint. Colour needs images in original rendering, drawn per account and per light / dark scheme; whether the iOS 27 rail honours that is unverified. The build starts with a one-hour spike on the Duo simulator: colour kept → B + C; stripped → B alone.
