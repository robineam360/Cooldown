Android account switcher — top tabs, bottom bar or rail?

Design for Robin, 2026-11-13 · Fable (expertise) · answers "is it right to continue using tabs at the top on Android when iOS has the switcher in the bottom and the Duo has it in the right? Let's go as per the best design on the native platforms." Drawn at 1 dp = 1 px at 360 / 412 / 750 / 840 dp, light and dark, with 2 / 4 / 10 accounts, every account tab wearing the CCRM-118 (Header Glyphs) icon (accent mark, person / briefcase badge, name; B0 of design/2026-11-13-tag-badge-styles.html). Panels marked light / dark are forced. Today's code: MainActivity.kt ≈ 777–875 (strip + HorizontalPager), ui/ProfileTabStrip.kt (fixed ↔ scrolling by measured labels, CCBG-81 (History Tab Wrap)), HistoryScreen.kt 120–157 (the same strip, no All). CCRM-116 (Inner Panes), CCRM-117 (Fold Panes), CCRM-118 (Header Glyphs), CCBG-86 (Rail Marks Alike).

Short answer: yes, keep the tabs at the top on Android. The accounts are peer views of one "Usage" screen, and Material 3's component for peer views is tabs — with swipe. iOS's bottom bar and the Duo's rail are iOS's native containers for the same role (SwiftUI's TabView is the only system switcher there), not a cross-platform rule. Material's bottom bar and rail are for destinations (Usage, History, Settings), and the app would have to turn its three screens into destinations to use them honestly.

Recommendation: T, unchanged, for v2.0 — and not a v2.0 item at all. Android's strip already is the Material answer; the one visible change due is the CCRM-118 icon on its tabs, already in review. Parity with iOS is in the icon (same accent mark + badge on the iPhone bar, the Duo rail, the iPad sidebar and Android's strip), not in where the strip sits. A bottom navigation bar (B) fails at one or two accounts (a bar needs three destinations), overflows at six (M3 has no "More" — that is an iOS pattern), and takes 80 dp + the gesture inset from every page; it also has no swipe, which is the real reach answer on Android (a swipe anywhere on the page switches accounts; the strip need never be reached). A rail (R) on the Fold's inner screen spends 80 dp of the one screen wide enough for a good chart (the 2026-08-04 one-column decision) and collides with CCRM-117 (Fold Panes), which wants that width for Usage | Pace. The top-app-bar picker (E) hides the thing people open the app to switch. The destinations bar (D: Usage · History · Settings at the bottom, account tabs under the top bar) is the only option that is more Material than today; it is a restructure of three screens, after v2.0, only if the beta says the toolbar icons are hard to find. Desktop impact: none.

What Android apps with accounts as peers do: none of the ones I know put accounts in the bottom bar. Google's apps keep the destinations in the bar and the account behind the avatar (one account "in use", switching is rare). Slack keeps workspaces in a leading column on wide windows and behind the Home header on phones. X and Telegram keep accounts in the drawer. All of these treat the account as a context you set and forget; Cooldown is the opposite — switching accounts is the use ("how much is left on the one I'm about to spend"), which is why the visible strip with a swipe beats every hidden switcher. 1 · What the accounts are, and what History and Settings are

  • Accounts = peer views (data sets) of one "Usage" destination. Every account page is the same screen with different numbers: same cards, same toolbar, same History, same Settings. The All tab is the Summary of the same data. Nothing about an account is a "section of the app".
  • History and Settings = secondary screens reached by top-app-bar actions, with system Back walking home (BackHandler). Material allows this: a single primary destination with secondary screens needs no navigation bar at all (Clock-style apps with a bar are the ones with several primary sections). They would become destinations only under option D.
  • Consequence: Material's container for "same content, other data set, at the top of the content pane, swipeable" is the tab row. Android already has the native answer; the question is really whether reach (thumb) justifies breaking the rule. It does not, because the swipe is the reach answer and it exists only on T.

2 · The icon used on every panel

Android 24 dp box: provider mark 20 dp at the top-left in the account accent; a 13 dp disc cleared 1.5 dp wider out of the lower right (surface colour, so it is right on the ivory Claude room and in dark); the filled person / briefcase 8 dp in ink. Unselected tab: the name at 60 %, the icon never dims (a colour at 60 % is another hue). The All tab: three bars, 20 dp, ink.

3 · T — today's top tabs, with the new icon

Fixed equal shares while every label (icon 24 + 6 + name, All included, plus the tab's 32 dp padding) fits its share; scrolling with each tab at its label (min 90 dp) once one would not. 360 dp with All + 2 is fixed; All + 4 scrolls on every phone; ten always scroll, and the strip's scroll follows the pager. Swipe anywhere on the page switches accounts — the strip is never the only control. History shows the same strip without All (drawn once, §7).

4 · B — a Material 3 navigation bar at the bottom

80 dp tall bar plus the gesture-navigation inset the bar extends under (24 dp drawn; 48 dp with three-button navigation). All + accounts as destinations: 3 items at two accounts, 5 at four, and at ten the two honest drawings — what Compose's NavigationBar does with 11 items (each 32 dp wide, labels cut) and a custom "More" destination (All + 3 + More, the rest in a sheet, iOS's pattern, not Material's). The top strip is gone; the swipe is gone with it (a navigation bar's destinations do not page). One account: no bar at all, as no strip today. On 750 / 840 Material says a bar is wrong — it becomes the rail (§5) — so B is drawn only at 360 / 412.

5 · R — a navigation rail on medium / expanded widths (T on compact)

80 dp rail at the leading edge (Material puts it left; the Duo's iOS rail is at the trailing edge — a difference Robin would see when he folds either phone), full height, items 56 dp with 12 dp between: All + accounts. Content column loses 80 dp: the Fold inner's chart goes from ≈ 678 to ≈ 598 dp; on a 840 dp window the 760 dp column still fits beside it. At ten accounts the eleven items need ≈ 740 dp of height: fine on the Fold inner (832), not on a landscape tablet (≈ 600) — the Compose rail does not scroll (UNVERIFIED whether Expressive's does). Swipe between accounts stays if the pager stays, but a rail selection and a pager page are then two models of one state. Compact widths keep T.

6 · E — an account picker in the top app bar (All as the home)

Google's pattern: the selected account's icon sits as the trailing "avatar"; tap opens a bottom sheet (All, then every account with its Weekly figure); the page is All by default. The strip and the swipe are gone; every switch is two taps; the account in view is said only by the 24 dp icon in the corner. Drawn at 412 dp, sheet open, four and ten accounts. It solves reach and ten accounts at once, and it hides the one thing the app is for.

7 · D — the destinations bar: Usage · History · Settings at the bottom, account tabs on top

The only option that makes the app more Material than today: three real destinations in a navigation bar, and the account tabs (secondary tabs, swipeable) under the top bar on Usage and on History — the same strip as today. Settings keeps its four tabs. Reach improves for History and Settings (today two 48 dp icons at the top right), not for accounts. Costs a back-stack and deep-link rework, the toolbar's redesign, every screenshot and the surfaces file. Drawn at 412 and 750, four accounts; and History's strip (no All) as it is today and as it would stay.

9 · Questions for Robin — one decision each, my answer first

  1. Keep the account tabs at the top on Android? Yes. They are the Material 3 component for peer data sets, and they are the only option that keeps the swipe. The CCRM-118 icon lands on them; nothing else changes.
  2. Is this a v2.0 change? No — no change at all in v2.0. If any of B / R / D is wanted later it is a new CCRM (allocated from the highest number in ROADMAP.md), after v2.0, wireframe first under CLAUDE.md §2; this page is the research, not the wireframe.
  3. Should History and Settings become bottom destinations (D) after v2.0? Not unless the beta says so. File it only if testers report not finding History or Settings; reach for accounts is solved by the swipe already.
  4. A rail on the Fold 7 inner screen? No. That width goes to Usage | Pace panes (CCRM-117 (Fold Panes)), and a rail of accounts is a rail of data sets, not destinations.
  5. Keep the swipe between accounts on Android even though iOS lost it on 2026-11-02? Yes. iOS lost it as the price of the system TabView; Android's system component for this role keeps it. Losing it for symmetry would make Android worse to match a constraint Android does not have.

Desktop impact: none — the desktop's switcher is its own surface and nothing here touches spec/core; the shared part is the CCRM-118 icon, which the desktop session sees with the frozen pages (Robin 2026-11-11).
