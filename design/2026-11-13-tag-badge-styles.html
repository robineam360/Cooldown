Recommendation: keep B0. Same colour as the provider? No — the badge is a second mark drawn on the mark; it reads only by contrasting with it, and ink is the one colour that contrasts with all twelve account accents in both schemes (B2 shows what an accent badge does: it reads as a leftover piece of the mark). No circular border? It has one: the 1.5 pt cleared gap around the disc is a ring in the surface colour, drawn by subtraction, so it is right on every background (the Claude tab's ivory, dark mode, a card). A painted 1 pt ring (B1) adds a line inside a 13 pt disc that already holds a 9 pt figure, and leaves 1 pt of air. A colour per tag? Drawn as B3, honestly, and I advise against it on three counts: the twelve theme accents cover the whole hue circle, so any tag colour equals some account's accent (the pair proposed below is the Purple and Cyan themes); red–green colour-blindness leaves only the blue–yellow axis, and yellow / orange / red are the severity ladder; and an 8 pt figure has too little area to carry a colour that is meant to be identified, not just noticed. If Robin wants a second channel beyond the figure, B4 (round for Personal, rounded square for Work) is the one that keeps the never-a-colour rule, costs no legibility and needs no change elsewhere.

0 · The standing rule, and what revisiting it for the badge would mean

BEHAVIOR-MOBILE §6 (CCRM-115 (Row Tag Glyph), Robin 2026-11-11): the row glyph is "in the muted ink, never a colour, never the word". The reason recorded with CCRM-110 (Account Tags) on 2026-11-10: the app already spends colour twice — accents mean provider / account (Claude orange, ChatGPT green, Gemini blue, and any of the twelve themes per account) and severity colours mean usage (yellow > 80, orange > 90, red at 100). A third colour meaning has nowhere to live. Relaxing it "for the badge only" is not possible in practice: the badge, the Summary row glyph, the Settings chip, the filter segments and the share snapshot all show the same tag, and a Personal that is violet in the tab strip and ink in the row below it is one tag drawn two ways. So B3 is a decision about every tag surface on both platforms and the desktop, not about the badge.

0.1 The palette the tag colours would have to avoid

Any hue picked for a tag sits within a few degrees of a theme accent an account may already wear. The pair drawn in B3 — Personal violet #7E57C2 / dark #B39DDB, Work teal #00838F / dark #4DD0E1 — is the best I can find: off the three provider hues, off the severity ladder, 5.2:1 and 4.5:1 against a white figure in light, and distinct in lightness as well as hue so the colour-blind rows keep some difference. It still equals the Purple and Cyan themes, and under deuteranopia both drift towards blue (§3).
