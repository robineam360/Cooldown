The row surfaces: Summary rows, the snapshot, Settings.

2 · Problem 2 — the All · Personal · Work control

2.0 Why "Work" reads greyed (for Robin, from the code)

It is the designed state, not a bug. A segment whose group has no accounts is drawn at 38% (ui/AccountTags.kt, SummaryFilterControl: .alpha(if (live) 1f else 0.38f)), and live means "at least one account carries that tag" (data/AccountTag.kt, SummaryFilters.enabled). Every account that existed before v2.0, and every account added without choosing Work, is Personal: AccountTag.read maps anything but "work" to Personal, silently (ROADMAP CCRM-110, "accounts that exist at the upgrade … are Personal, silently"). So on your phone no account is tagged Work, the Work group is empty, and the segment dims; a tap on it shows "No Work accounts yet. Tag one in Settings." for 3 s (addendum T3, your pick B). The sample looks different because its Teams and Lab are Work. Tag any account Work (Settings › the account's ⋮ › Tag…) and the segment lights up. The colour is right (ink at 38%, the same dim as the Add sheet's disabled segments). Glyphs on their own would not change this: a briefcase at 38% reads just as "off" as the word does. What reads wrong is the thing underneath — a filter with one usable choice (All and Personal are the same list) shown permanently. The drawings below fix the symptom (glyphs) and the cause (the gate) separately so you can take either.

2.1 Today — 360 dp phone, list 288 dp, straight after the upgrade (every account Personal)

Today costs: 149 dp of header; a permanently dim segment for everyone who never tags a Work account (most users, and Robin today); the sort glyph on 360 dp phones and, by the 320 dp rule, on the Fold's inner screen too.

2.2 Glyph segments, dim kept — All · person · briefcase

Segments 35 · 36 · 36 (10 dp padding, a 16 dp glyph in ink, the chosen one tonal as now); 111 dp in all, 38 fewer. "All" stays a word: it has no glyph the rows teach. The words stay as the accessibility labels. The glyphs are the ones on the rows directly below, so the control is its own key: tap the person, the rows with a person remain.

Glyph-only costs: a glyph to learn (taught by the rows and the Settings chips, which show glyph + word); both platforms change together; the dim question is untouched.

2.3 Glyph + count

Glyph + count costs: 22 dp over glyph-only; numbers in the header; "All 3" would be redundant so All stays bare, which makes the row uneven.

2.4 The gate — no control while only one tag is in use (recommended, with 2.2)

Rev B showed the control "once any account has a tag"; the 2026-11-11 model gave every account a tag, which turned that gate into "always". The same intent in the new model is: show the control once both tags are in use (at least one Personal and one Work account). Then every segment is always live, so the 38% state, the tap note and TalkBack's "no accounts" never occur, and the code loses them. With one tag in use the header is v1.9's "Weekly … your order ▾". The memory rule is unchanged (a stored value whose group empties is rewritten to All; the control disappearing is the same event). Discovery stays where it is: the Add sheet's "This account is Personal / Work" with its caption "Groups it on the Summary", and the Tag dialog's line "Tags group accounts on the Summary: All · Personal · Work."

Gate costs: a change to the shared rule (BEHAVIOR-MOBILE §6 controlShown, the tags fixture both clients test, core SummaryFilter.resolve), so iOS builds it too; the 3-s note, the 38% alpha and TalkBack's "no accounts" are removed rather than redrawn; a user with one tag never sees the filter until a second kind of account exists; it makes addendum T3 B moot (Robin's call, question 7).

2.5 Narrow and large text (glyph segments)

Rule: the glyph is 16 dp at font scale 1.0 and grows with the font (16 × scale), so large-text users do not get a tiny icon beside a large "All". The control wraps under the title from scale 1.5 as today, its three segments sharing the width equally (today they share by word length, 3 : 8 : 4, which the glyphs no longer need). The sort button shows its words whenever they fit the measured line (the iOS rule), replacing Android's "glyph under 320 dp".
