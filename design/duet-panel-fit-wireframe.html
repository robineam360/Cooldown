CCBG-26 (Panel Scaling) — option A approved and built · 2026-09-10

The expanded Duet panel never scales: what shows when a strip appears

The always-on notification's expanded view draws its lower half — the condition strips and the Weekly rows — as one bitmap and hands it to an ImageView that keeps the aspect ratio. When the bitmap is taller than the room Android leaves under the two header blocks, the whole bitmap is shrunk uniformly: the strips lose width, the Weekly rows lose width and height, and the text lands at about 60% of its size. With no strip the bitmap is short enough to fit, so the same rows draw at full width. That is the whole of the difference Robin saw between the two shots. This wireframe shows the fix — draw the panel as native views so it can only ever be full width — and the one decision it forces: what gives way when the strips and the meters do not both fit.

1 · What is happening

Mechanism. drawPanel renders a bitmap at a nominal 340 dp width and whatever height its content needs; notif_duet_expanded.xml shows it with adjustViewBounds + fitStart. Android caps the expanded custom view (on the Fold 7 that leaves about 136 dp under the headers; on stock Android nearer 120), so when the bitmap needs more, the ImageView scales it down to fit the height and the width follows. Two things compound it: the approved height budget priced a strip at 44–59 dp (title only, or title and a one-line sub) but drawPanel wraps the detail to two lines, so a real strip costs 75 dp; and the bar budget (4 − strips) still draws both Weekly rows beside two strips although the approved table already said "at 2 strips the panel is full, no bar fits". Nothing ever checked the height, so nothing gave way — the bitmap just got smaller.

2 · The fix that both options share: native rows, so width is never in play

The panel stops being a bitmap. Each Weekly row becomes what the header blocks already are — TextViews at their real sp and a 13 dp bar ImageView with fitXY (the approved CCRM-62 (Duet Notification) technique, which is why the header bars in both shots above are full width while the panel is not). A strip becomes a row with a tinted rounded background, a 6 dp dot and text. Nothing can be scaled any more: a row is exactly as tall as its dp say, at whatever width the skin gives the card. What still has to be decided is what the fixed height budget holds, because two two-line strips and two Weekly rows cost 230 dp and no phone has that. Both options below are drawn for the tight case, ~120 dp; a skin that gives more simply shows the same content with air under it.

3 · Option A · the meters stay, the strips go to one line recommended

The Weekly rows are the reason the expanded panel exists, so they are never displaced. On a Duet a strip is one line: the dot, then the condition's short text (the form written for the collapsed row: "Product: Sign-in stopped working", "Product: Stale — nothing since 6:00 am", "Update available — v1.7"). The two-line detail moves to the app, which the tap already opens. Only one strip is drawn; a second and beyond fold into a "+ n more" tail on that line instead of a separate line, because a separate line (27 dp) is what would push the second Weekly row out. Model-cap rows leave the Duet panel: with 120 dp there is no row left for them once the two Weekly rows are in, and today they only ever appeared shrunk. They stay in the app and in the single-account layout.

5 · What else this touches

  • Single-account layout (Second = None). Same bitmap, same fitStart, so it scales too once it overflows — with its ~180 dp of panel room that is two two-line strips plus the 71 dp Weekly row (223), or three strips alone (216). It gets the native rendering in the same change with its content rules untouched, which turns the shrink into a bottom clip in those states. Whether its cap should fall from 3 to 2 is a follow-on question, not asked here.
  • Strip background. RemoteViews cannot tint a shape drawable per hue, so the strip is a FrameLayout with a rounded-rect ImageView behind the text, coloured with setColorFilter and setImageAlpha(33) — the 13% the bitmap used. Three hues (red, amber, accent) as today.
  • Wording. Nothing new. The one-line strip uses the short string every condition already has for the collapsed row; "+ n more" is the existing overflow phrase minus its "— open the app for the rest" clause, which the tap makes redundant.
  • Tap targets. Unchanged: the panel falls to First's Cooldown tab, each header block to its own account.
  • Screenshots. The README, the User Guide (page 9) and the brochure all use v16-notif-expanded.png, which is the shrunk state. All three are retaken after this lands.

6 · Decisions

  1. Option A — approved by Robin, 2026-09-10. Built the same day as CCBG-26 (Panel Scaling): notif_panel_duet.xml, notif_panel_single.xml, PinnedNotification.fillPanel; Duet.maxStrips(true) is 1.
  2. Native rows for both layouts — follows from either answer; not itself a design choice.
  3. Model caps leave the Duet panel — follows from either answer (they never fit unshrunk).
