CCRM-61 (Settings Diet) · CCRM-62 (Duet Notification) — proposed

A shorter Settings screen, and one notification that shows two accounts

Every home-screen widget goes, every standalone notification goes except the reset ping, and the four pinned styles collapse to the one that is actually run: Huge number. What is left is one always-on notification carrying two accounts, and a Settings screen of four swipeable tabs instead of thirteen stacked sections. This wireframe draws Settings before and after, then draws the two-account notification in nine states so the layout can be judged before any of it is built.

1 · Settings, before and after

Thirteen sections become four swipeable tabs. The mocks are the real row order at the real type sizes; struck-through rows in the "before" column are the ones that go. Everything the dropped rows controlled either stops existing or becomes a strip in the pinned panel, which CCRM-44 (One Surface) already built. One thing on the Notifications list survives, and it is the one that could not become a strip: reset pings.

what is wrong with it

Thirteen section headings in one column, and the two that dominate it, Notifications and Pinned notification, are 26 of the 43 rows between them. On the Fold 7 cover screen that is roughly four screens of scrolling to reach Appearance and six to reach About. The inner screen already splits it into two columns, which helps the tablet case and does nothing for the phone one.

The split it uses today is by subject: what the accounts are on one side, how this device surfaces them on the other. That is a reasonable division and it is invisible, because a section heading looks the same whichever column it lands in.

After · four swipeable tabs approved

A fixed TabRow of four tabs above a HorizontalPager, which is exactly the pattern the main screen's ProfileTabs already uses: tap a tab or swipe horizontally to move between them. Drawn at 410 dp, the Fold 7 cover width.

Tab-width arithmetic. Four fixed tabs across 410 dp is 102.5 dp each. Measured at 14 sp: "Accounts" 63 dp, "Alerts" 39, "Appearance" 81, "More" 35. Material's fixed tab wants 16 dp of padding a side, which leaves 70.5 dp of text room, so "Appearance" needs the padding down to 10 dp a side to fit without truncating: 102.5 − 20 = 82.5 dp against its 81, which is the whole margin, one and a half dp. "Notifications" measures 85 dp and cannot be made to fit at all, which is why that tab is called Alerts. If 10 dp padding reads too tight on a device, the fallback is a scrollable TabRow, which costs the "all four visible at once" property that is the point of doing this.

The trade-offs, both directions.
For. Each tab fits one cover screen without scrolling, which no arrangement of a flat list does. The four groups match how often each is touched: Accounts when an account changes, Alerts when the notification changes, Appearance rarely, More almost never. And the tab bar is a standing table of contents, where thirteen headings in a scroll are not.
Against. A horizontal swipe inside Settings must not fight horizontally scrolling content. It does not here: the theme colour grid and every chip row are FlowRows, which wrap rather than scroll, so there is no horizontal scroller anywhere inside a tab to steal the gesture. The real cost is that every setting is one tap further away than in a flat list, and a setting whose tab you have forgotten is worse to find than one you can scroll past. Search would fix that and there is none.
Honest caveat. The Alerts tab fits one cover screen with two accounts. A third account adds two more chip-row pairs and it scrolls. Approved in review with that known.

43 rows before, across 13 sections. 24 after, across four tabs. 19 rows go, 4 merge down into 3, 1 moves, 19 stay as they are, and 1 is new: "Status-bar ring shows". The Duet's Second chip row rides inside Show accounts and adds no row of its own.

The consequence, stated plainly. With the always-on notification switched off, the app posts nothing except the reset pings you have switched on. No sound, no heads-up, no status-bar ring, no sign-in warning. Sign-in trouble shows as the status chip on the Accounts card, and as a strip in the pinned panel when the notification is on. Channels deleted: usage_alerts, auth_alerts, health_alerts, ping_alerts, pace_alerts, update_alerts. Two stay: pinned_usage_v2 and reset_alerts.
Why reset pings are the exception. Every other alert on that list describes a state, and a state can be read off a panel whenever you next look. "Your window just reset" is momentary: it is true for a minute and then it is not, so there is nothing to fold and nothing to read later. That makes it the one standalone notification left, and it posts even while the always-on notification is on, which is the one exception to the CCRM-44 (One Surface) silence contract. The fold-into-panel machinery for it goes with the other event strips. resetPingMode becomes per account, so one account can ping and the other stay quiet.
Which strips survive. The event strips, threshold crossings and pace milestones, go with the alerts that produced them: there is no event any more. The reset ping keeps no strip either, because it is a notification again. The three condition strips stay, because a condition is state rather than an alert and the panel is the only place that state is legible: sign-in stopped working, data is stale, update available.
Quick Settings tile: dropped outright. Not just the section, the tile. Android tints tile icons monochrome, so a tile cannot carry the account-identity colour the status-bar ring now depends on, which made it the one surface that could not say whose number it was showing. Four TileService declarations, their shared implementation and their icon-style read all go, and CCRM-11 (Tile Reset Time) is superseded. Tiles already placed on a device disappear when the update installs, the same as the widgets.

2 · The Duet notification (CCRM-62 (Duet Notification))

The always-on notification carries two accounts instead of one. Everything below is drawn on the dark shade at the Fold 7 cover width: shade content 360 dp, notification card 360 dp wide with the 16 dp padding Android applies, corner radius 24 dp, 1 dp drawn as 1 px. Only the Huge number style is in play, because it is the only one built from custom RemoteViews and therefore the only one with two number slots to give. The other three styles put their single figure in the system's large-icon slot, which there is exactly one of.

The width arithmetic. 360 dp card, minus 16 dp of padding each side, leaves 328 dp of drawable width. One 16 dp gutter down the middle leaves 156 dp per half. If a skin gives the custom view the full card width the halves grow to 172 dp, so 156 is the number every clamp below is set from: the tight case has to work. Inside a half, a three-character figure at 30 sp bold takes about 54 dp, plus an 8 dp gap. That leaves a 90 dp left column, and the 14 dp mark plus its 4 dp gap live inside it too, so the label clamps at about 72 dp.
The height constraint on the collapsed row. A collapsed custom view gets roughly 48 to 64 dp depending on the skin. Today's single row already spends about 53 dp: a 19 dp label line, a 13 dp bar with its margin, and a 16 dp reset sub. Two of those do not fit, at any width. Something has to leave, and the three options below differ only in what.

Expanded · one layout

Two header blocks stacked, then the panel bitmap under them. Each header block is the mark at 14 dp, the account and window name at 14 sp, a 13 dp bar with the tick, the reset sub at 12 sp in the CCRM-23 (Reset Display) order, and the figure trailing at 36 sp, down from today's 44 so that two of them fit. The panel keeps its existing grammar and gains an account prefix on every row, because with two accounts in the header a bare window name no longer says whose.

Two accounts doubled every label in this view, and the prefix has to come from somewhere. "5h" is what the account cards and the reset-ping rows in Settings now say too, so the whole app uses one pair of names for the two windows.

panel rules

Weekly rows. An account whose headline is the 5h window gets a <account> · Weekly row in the panel. An account whose headline is already the weekly window gets no second row, which is the existing skipWeeklyBar rule from CCRM-54 (ChatGPT Account) part 2, now applied per account rather than once for the whole panel.

Model caps. Same prefix, same shared budget: drawPanel allows four bars minus one for every strip on screen, and the two Weekly rows now take two of those four before any cap is drawn. In practice a Duet panel with one strip shows the two Weekly rows and nothing else.

Strips. Unchanged from CCRM-44 (One Surface): label-prefixed with the account, newest first, red conditions above amber, update last, and an overflow line. The cap has to fall from three to two, for the reason in the height budget below.

Refresh. The action stays on the notification, one button for both accounts. It already refreshes every account, so there is nothing to split.

Height budget

Android caps the expanded custom view at about 256 dp. Two header blocks and their gaps spend more than half of it, which is the real constraint on this design and the reason the panel row has to be recut.

what the ring says, and what it stops saying

Arc is the 5h spend, needle is even pace, drawn in the CCRM-51 (Rails Gauge) grammar. What is gone is the weekly reading: below 80% the hue is the account, so orange means Claude and green means ChatGPT at a glance. Above 80% severity takes the hue back, and identity is lost for as long as the window is nearly spent, which is the accepted cost. At that point the number matters more than whose it is, and one tap opens the shade where both halves are labelled.

This is now the app's only glyph. The Quick Settings tile is dropped, and the reason is exactly this ring: Android tints tile icons monochrome, so a tile could carry the fill but never the colour, and without the colour it cannot say which account it is showing.

why not a twin glyph

Because it was tried and withdrawn. CCRM-49 (Glyph Legibility) measured a concentric two-ring "Twin" at about 14 dp and could not read it, and the status-bar slot is fitted by width at roughly 15 dp, so there is no more room to be had. Dropping the hub dot buys back the only interior room the ring had, and it buys it for legibility rather than for a second reading.

New Settings row: "Status-bar ring shows", chips First / Second / Whichever is higher. The third picks the account with the higher 5h percentage and may switch during the day. That is deliberate: the colour change is the tell, so the glyph swapping from orange to green is itself the information that the other account has taken the lead.

3 · What leaves with the widgets and the alerts

Categories, not a file list: the exact inventory is being audited separately. This is here so the size of the deletion is on the table before it is approved, not after.

  • Five widget providers and everything that registers them: the receivers in the manifest, their xml/ provider-info files, and their preview drawables. The tall usage widget, the single-bar widget, CCRM-39 (Ring Widget), CCRM-40 (Mini-Rings Widget) and CCRM-41 (Pace Widget).
  • The widget configuration activity and its layout, which is CCRM-4 (Widget Quick-Edit).
  • The Glance dependency and everything that exists only to satisfy it, including the Compose-for-Glance theming shims.
  • The widget redraw worker, and the refreshWidgets() calls threaded through Settings, the repository and the poll path. That is a lot of call sites on surfaces that are staying, so this is the part most likely to leave dead parameters behind.
  • The widget-specific settings: "Show on widgets" under Usage credits, "On widgets" under Show red past the pace mark, and the two "Add to home screen" links.
  • Most of the standalone alert evaluator, and six of its seven channels: usage_alerts, auth_alerts, health_alerts, ping_alerts, pace_alerts, update_alerts. With them go the group-summary safety net, the alert-lifetime store, the standalone update notification, and CCRM-21 (Pace Alerts) as a notifying feature. The dormant CCRM-17 (Window Pings) code goes with its channel, having never been enabled. What stays is the reset-ping path and its reset_alerts channel, plus the reset scheduler that fires it. That is the one branch of the evaluator with a caller left, so the file shrinks rather than disappearing.
  • The fold-into-panel machinery for events: the strip store, the strip-lifetime alarm from CCBG-18 (Strip Lifetime Stamp), and the event side of the strip stack. The three condition strips keep their side of it.
  • The Quick Settings tile: UsageTileService and the four thin subclasses that give it four tiles, all four <service> declarations in the manifest with their BIND_QUICK_SETTINGS_TILE permission and QS_TILE intent filters, the tile's read of the status-bar icon style, and its own reset-form read. That retires CCRM-11 (Tile Reset Time), which existed only to give the tile a countdown-or-clock choice before Appearance took the setting over.
  • Three retired pinned styles, Gauge, Number tile and Progress bar, with their bitmap drawing and their BigPictureStyle path, and three retired status-bar glyphs, Pie, Battery and Number. Plus the ring's hub dot, which retires CCRM-50 (Weekly Flag) with it.
  • Two of the three pace-mark toggles: the widget one goes with the widgets, and the remaining two collapse into one, so paceOverOnWidgets and one of paceOverInApp / paceOverOnNotification go too.
  • Docs and images: the README, guide and brochure sections that describe widgets, the widget screenshots in release/docs/, and the chart-widget mention left over from CCRM-13 (Chart Widget). The Settings screenshots all need retaking as four tabs.
Say this in the release notes. Widgets already placed on a home screen disappear when the update installs: the provider is gone, so the launcher drops the cell, and some launchers leave a blank gap until they prune it. There is no migration and no warning the app can post, because the app is not running when the install happens. Quick Settings tiles already placed disappear too, now that the tile services go with them: the OS drops a tile whose service no longer exists, and a user who had put one in their shade will simply find it gone. Nothing else the app has ever placed outside itself survives this update, so the release note can say it once and cover both.
One migration is needed. resetPingMode is a single global pair of values today and becomes a per-account pair. The honest default is to copy the existing global value onto every account, so a user who had the 5h ping on keeps it on everywhere and can then turn it off per account. The pace-mark collapse needs no migration worth the name: on if either surviving toggle was on is the honest read, since the user asked for red somewhere.
