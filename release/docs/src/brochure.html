Claude and ChatGPT both meter you on a 5-hour session window and a 7-day weekly one. Cooldown keeps them in your Android shade — two accounts on one always-on notification, a ring in the status bar, as many accounts as you use on either service — forecasts when you'll hit the wall at your current pace, and pings you the moment you're back.

What it does

📌Two accounts, one notificationAlways-on and silent: a First and a Second account side by side, each with its mark, bar and big percentage.
◎The status-bar ringA tiny ring fills with the 5-hour window, in the account's own colour so you know whose it is.
📈The pace chartEvery reading plotted, with a forecast tail: "At this pace: 100% at Thu 2:40 PM — 1h 20m before the reset."
🔔Reset pingsPer account and per window — Off, If busy, or Always — so you know the moment you're back.
⏱️The even-pace lineOne diagonal across each chart, one tick on every bar. Below it you'll finish inside your limit.
👥Claude and ChatGPTPick the service when you add an account, then mix as many as you like — each in its own room: ivory and serif for Claude, neutral for ChatGPT.
🔐Sign in on the phoneClaude uses the OAuth flow Claude Code does; ChatGPT takes a short code you approve at auth.openai.com. No computer, no copied tokens.
💳Usage creditsPay-as-you-go credits get their own card — spent, total and what's left, or a plain balance on ChatGPT. Plans without them never see it.
💾Private & backed upNo servers or telemetry; the token is Keystore-encrypted. History & settings survive a reinstall (token excluded).

How it was made

Cooldown exists because of one recurring pain: deep in a Claude chat, a Cowork run or a ChatGPT session, the dreaded "approaching your limit" — with no idea how close, or when the window resets. Claude Code can tell you; the chat apps can't. The fix: put the answer where your eyes already are.

Claude Fable 5 built it — in a weekend. After a week of throwaway prototypes, one weekend turned it real: pushed to git, signed, running on a phone. Fable wrote virtually everything — the app, the notification, the alert logic, the reverse-engineered usage API.

Then it hit the one wall Fable wouldn't cross: the OAuth sign-in handshake, which Fable declined on cybersecurity grounds. Claude Opus 4.8 stepped in — built the native sign-in the app now uses, and wrote the docs Fable left undone. Claude Opus 5 shipped v1.1's pace chart and credits, then v1.5's ChatGPT accounts on the same rails; Claude Fable 5.1 ran v1.6 — the two-account notification and the Pulse tile. If you pay for Claude, you already have the engineer… or four.

Want it on your phone?

It's free & open-source. Scan the code (or visit github.com/robineam360/Cooldown), grab the latest APK from the Releases page, and follow the detailed user guide — install → sign-in → notification in ~3 minutes. Android 12+ today; iOS is on the roadmap.

And if the "built by Claude" part is what got you curious — even better. Bring an idea of your own; it takes fewer evenings than you'd think.
